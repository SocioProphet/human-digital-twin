The light cone of time & uncertainty

How the simulation represents time, causality, and uncertainty at its most fundamental level: a universal process of spherical symmetry forming and breaking. The convex outer surface carries positive charge / emission; the concave inner surface carries negative charge / absorption; the apex is a continuous exchange of energy — the thing the twin measures as time.

The 4π sphere of uncertainty

Two surfaces across one time axis

The two-dimensional surface of the sphere runs across the vertical time axis; the time axis sits at right angles to the space axes because electromagnetic fields are always mutually perpendicular. Symmetry forms and breaks continuously — a dynamic boundary condition, never a static state.

Because the universe is never at absolute zero, this exchange never stops — which is exactly why the twin can treat time as a measured, continuous process rather than a fixed coordinate.

Heisenberg at the smallest scale

At the finest grain the sphere of uncertainty is bounded by the uncertainty principle — position and momentum cannot both be pinned:

Δx · Δp > ħ4π

the 4π is the full solid angle of the sphere the twin samples over

The apex is where three clocks are read at once

The conceptual light cone above is realised as a running instrument. Every observation the twin makes is stamped on three notions of time; the disagreement between them is the uncertainty sphere, made numeric — and the reading fails closed when there isn't enough evidence to trust it.

wall
physical clock — the space axis of the diagram
causal

ε_order

staleness

ε_rate

ε_phase
